\section{The multipath channel}
In a radio channel the transmitted signal reaches the receiver along many paths with
different delays $\tau_l$, complex gains and Doppler shifts. The low-pass equivalent channel is
a linear time-varying filter,
\begin{equation}
  y(t)=\sum_l a_l(t)\,x(t-\tau_l)+w(t),\qquad h(\tau;t)=\sum_l a_l(t)\,\delta(\tau-\tau_l).
\end{equation}
Two scales characterize it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{delay spread} $\sigma_\tau$, whose inverse is the coherence bandwidth
  $B_c\approx1/(5\sigma_\tau)$;
  \item the maximum Doppler shift $f_D=v f_c/c$, whose inverse sets the coherence time
  $T_c\approx0.423/f_D$.
\end{itemize}
A signal of bandwidth $B\ll B_c$ sees \emph{flat} fading (a single complex gain). If $B\gtrsim
B_c$ the fading is frequency selective and causes ISI.

\section{Rayleigh and Rician fading}
When a path is the superposition of many unresolved scatterers with random phases, the
central limit theorem makes its gain circular complex Gaussian, $h\sim\CN(0,1)$. The envelope
$|h|$ is then Rayleigh distributed and the power $|h|^2$ exponential. A line-of-sight
component of relative power $K$ (the Rician factor) adds a deterministic term:
\begin{equation}
  h=\sqrt{\frac{K}{K+1}}\,e^{j\theta_0}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{K+1}}\,h_{\mathrm{Ray}},\qquad \E|h|^2=1 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Time correlation: the Clarke--Jakes model}
For isotropic scattering in the horizontal plane, a path arriving at angle $\alpha$ is shifted
by $f_D\cos\alpha$. Averaging over a uniform $\alpha$ gives~\cite{clarke1968} the autocorrelation and the U-shaped
Doppler spectrum
\begin{equation}
  R_h(\tau)=\E\big[h(t+\tau)h\conj(t)\big]=J_0(2\pi f_D\tau),\qquad
  S_h(f)=\frac{1}{\pi f_D\sqrt{1-(f/f_D)^2}},\ |f|<f_D .
  \label{eq:jakes}
\end{equation}
LinkStudio generates it with the sum-of-sinusoids model of Zheng and Xiao~\cite{zheng2003}:
\begin{equation}
  h[n]=\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{M}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big(\cos\psi_i+j\sin\psi_i\big)\cos\!\big(2\pi f_D\cos\alpha_i\,n/f_s+\phi\big),
  \qquad \alpha_i=\frac{2\pi i-\pi+\theta}{4M},
\end{equation}
with $\theta$, $\phi$ and the $\psi_i$ independent and uniform on $[-\pi,\pi)$. Jakes' original
deterministic model is not stationary. Fully random arrival angles are stationary but need many
sinusoids before a single realization behaves like the ensemble. The Zheng--Xiao choice spreads
the $M$ angles evenly over a quarter circle with one random offset. The process is wide-sense
stationary, $E|h|^2=1$, the in-phase and quadrature parts are uncorrelated, and the
autocorrelation matches \cref{eq:jakes} closely with only $M=8$ to 16 sinusoids (16 by default).
The test suite checks $R_h$ against $J_0$ to within 0.02 and the I/Q cross-correlation.

\section{Tapped delay lines and 3GPP profiles}
\blk{multipath} implements $y(t)=\sum_l h_l(t)\,x(t-\tau_l)$ on the sample grid. A delay
$\tau_lf_s$ that is not an integer cannot be applied by shifting samples. Rounding it would merge
taps: at LTE rates (15.36--30.72\,MHz) the EPA taps at 30, 70, 90 and 110\,ns fall onto two
samples, and the frequency correlation of the channel changes. The block therefore applies each
path through a band-limited fractional delay: a Hann-windowed sinc of $\pm8$ samples, normalized
to unit energy. Over 75\,\% of the Nyquist band it delays by the exact fractional amount within
5\,\% (checked in the tests). Integer delays stay single taps, and \prm{fractional_delays}\code{=false}
restores rounding. The profile is normalized to unit total power. Taps are Rayleigh (or static
with \prm{fading}\code{=false}), constant per frame or each with an independent Doppler
process. The 3GPP extended models of TS~36.104~\cite{3gpp36104} are built in:

Their RMS delay spreads are 43, 357 and 991\,ns.

\paragraph{5G NR profiles.} TR~38.901 \S7.7.2~\cite{tr38901} defines TDL-A, B and C (NLOS) and
TDL-D and E (LOS) with normalized delays: the tabulated profiles have unit RMS delay spread. The
delays are scaled to the wanted spread $\mathrm{DS}$ (\prm{delay_spread_ns}, from 10\,ns
``very short'' to 1000\,ns ``very long''), $\tau_n=\tau_{n,\mathrm{model}}\,\mathrm{DS}$. Computing
the RMS spread of the transcribed tables gives 1.000 for A, B, C and E and 0.994 for D, a check on
the 23--24 tap values that the tests repeat. In TDL-D and TDL-E the first tap holds a LOS path
(power $-0.2$\,dB and $-0.03$\,dB) on top of a Rayleigh component at the same delay. The block
gives the LOS path a fixed amplitude with random phase; in \prm{jakes} mode it also has the
Doppler shift $0.7f_D$ of the standard. The first tap is then Ricean with $K_1=13.3$\,dB (TDL-D) or
22\,dB (TDL-E). The tests estimate $K_1$ from 4000 frames and recover 13.3\,dB within 1\,dB.

The channel impulse response is published on \code{csi}: \meta{csi_type}\code{="cir"} with shape
$(B,L)$ for block fading, or \code{"cir_tv"} with shape $(B,L,n)$ for time-varying taps.
\codeexcerpt{delay_kernel}{Band-limited fractional delay used for every tap.}

\section{Path loss and link budgets}\label{sec:path-loss}
Fading describes fluctuations around a mean received power. The mean itself follows from the
distance through the path loss. \blk{path_loss} offers four models.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Free space} (ITU-R P.525~\cite{p525}):
  $L=20\log_{10}(4\pi d f_c/c)$, i.e. $32.45+20\log_{10}f_{\mathrm{MHz}}+20\log_{10}d_{\mathrm{km}}$\,dB.
  \item \textbf{UMa, UMi street canyon, InH office} (TR~38.901 Table 7.4.1-1~\cite{tr38901}), $f_c$ in
  GHz and $d_{3D}$ in metres. LOS uses a dual-slope law. In UMi, for example,
  \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{PL}_{\mathrm{LOS}}=\begin{cases}32.4+21\log_{10}d_{3D}+20\log_{10}f_c, & d_{2D}\le d'_{\mathrm{BP}}\\
    32.4+40\log_{10}d_{3D}+20\log_{10}f_c\\
    \quad-9.5\log_{10}\big(d'^2_{\mathrm{BP}}+(h_{\mathrm{BS}}-h_{\mathrm{UT}})^2\big), & d_{2D}>d'_{\mathrm{BP}}\end{cases}
  \end{equation}
  with breakpoint $d'_{\mathrm{BP}}=4h'_{\mathrm{BS}}h'_{\mathrm{UT}}f_c/c$ and effective heights
  $h'=h-1$\,m. This is the distance at which the ground-reflected ray starts cancelling the direct
  one, so the slope changes from about 2 to 4. NLOS is the larger of the LOS value and a
  steeper NLOS law, e.g. $35.3\log_{10}d_{3D}+22.4+21.3\log_{10}f_c-0.3(h_{\mathrm{UT}}-1.5)$ for UMi.
  Shadow fading is log-normal with $\sigma_{\mathrm{SF}}=4$/7.82\,dB (UMi LOS/NLOS), 4/6\,dB (UMa)
  and 3/8.03\,dB (InH).
\end{itemize}
With \prm{condition}\code{=probabilistic} each frame is LOS with the probability of Table 7.4.2-1,
e.g. $P_{\mathrm{LOS}}=18/d+e^{-d/36}(1-18/d)$ for UMi beyond 18\,m. The UMa breakpoint uses
$h_E=1$\,m, the standard's value for user heights below 13\,m.

\paragraph{Absolute noise.} A link budget needs the noise in watts. \blk{awgn} in
\prm{mode}\code{=thermal} adds $N_0=k_BTF$ per hertz, i.e. variance $k_BTF\,f_s$ per complex
sample, with noise figure $F$ (\prm{noise_figure_db}) and $T=290$\,K. The signal must then carry
absolute power. The mapper's unit power is read as 1\,W (30\,dBm) and the transmit power is set
with \blk{gain} (\prm{gain_db}\,=\,$P_{\mathrm{tx}}$[dBm]$-30$). At one sample per symbol
$E_s/N_0=P_{\mathrm{rx}}/(k_BTFR_s)$. A test runs QPSK at 7\,dBm over 2\,km of free space at
2.4\,GHz into a 5\,dB noise figure and checks the BER against theory at that $E_s/N_0$. The
\emph{Urban micro-cell link budget} scenario combines \blk{path_loss} and thermal noise: sweep the
distance and watch the LOS fraction and the frame error rate.

\section{Error probability with fading}\label{sec:rayleigh-ber}
With coherent detection and perfect channel knowledge, the instantaneous SNR of BPSK in flat
fading is $\gamma=|h|^2E_b/N_0$, exponentially distributed with mean $\bar\gamma$. Averaging
the AWGN error probability over it gives
\begin{equation}
  \bar P_b=\int_0^\infty\Q\big(\sqrt{2\gamma}\big)\,\frac{e^{-\gamma/\bar\gamma}}{\bar\gamma}\,d\gamma
  =\frac12\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\bar\gamma}{1+\bar\gamma}}\right)\ \xrightarrow{\ \bar\gamma\to\infty\ }\ \frac{1}{4\bar\gamma}.
  \label{eq:rayleigh-ber}
\end{equation}
The error rate now falls only as $1/\mathrm{SNR}$ instead of exponentially: 10\,dB more SNR
buys one decade of BER. Diversity, the subject of \cref{sec:diversity}, restores a steeper
slope. \code{ber_rayleigh} performs the same average numerically for any AWGN curve.
\Cref{fig:diversity} compares \cref{eq:rayleigh-ber} with the chain
\blk{flat_fading}$\to$\blk{awgn}$\to$\blk{one_tap_equalizer}.

\section{Equalization}\label{sec:equalization}
\subsection{One-tap equalization}
For flat fading, $y=hs+w$, and dividing by the known gain gives $\hat s=s+w/h$. The noise
variance becomes $\sigma^2/|h|^2$ symbol by symbol, and \blk{one_tap_equalizer} writes exactly
this array to \meta{noise_var}, so the soft demapper weights faded symbols correctly. The
\prm{phase} variant only derotates, which is sufficient for PSK.

\subsection{Linear FIR equalizers designed from the channel}
For a frequency-selective channel with impulse response $\vect h=(h_0,\dots,h_{L-1})$, a
linear equalizer $\vect w$ of $N$ taps produces the combined response $\mat H\vect w$, where
$\mat H$ is the $(N+L-1)\times N$ convolution matrix of $\vect h$. Zero forcing asks the combined
response to approximate a unit impulse $\vect e_d$ at decision delay $d$. The MMSE design adds
the noise power $\sigma^2$ as a regularizer:
\begin{equation}
  \vect w_d=\argmin_{\vect w}\ \|\mat H\vect w-\vect e_d\|^2+\lambda\|\vect w\|^2
  =(\mat H\Her\mat H+\lambda\mat I)^{-1}\mat H\Her\vect e_d,
  \qquad\lambda=\begin{cases}\sigma^2&\text{MMSE}\\ \approx0&\text{ZF.}\end{cases}
\end{equation}
All delays are solved at once, since the columns of $(\mat H\Her\mat H+\lambda\mat
I)^{-1}\mat H\Her$ are the solutions for every $d$. The delay with the largest useful gain
$(\mat H\vect w_d)_d$ is selected. The output is then scaled by $1/(\mat H\vect w_d)_d$ to remove
the MMSE bias, and the noise variance becomes $\sigma^2\|\vect w\|^2$.
\codeexcerpt{linear_eq}{Least-squares design of ZF/MMSE equalizers with delay selection (\blk{linear_equalizer}).}

\subsection{Adaptive equalization: LMS}
When the channel is unknown, the equalizer $z[n]=\vect w\Her\vect u[n]$, with
$\vect u[n]=(y[n],y[n-1],\dots)$, can minimize $\E|d[n]-z[n]|^2$ by stochastic gradient
descent. The instantaneous gradient with respect to $\vect w\conj$ is $-\vect u\,e\conj$ with
$e=d-z$, giving the LMS update and its normalized version:
\begin{equation}
  \vect w\leftarrow\vect w+\mu\,\vect u[n]\,e\conj[n],\qquad
  \mu_{\mathrm{NLMS}}=\frac{\tilde\mu}{\|\vect u[n]\|^2+\epsilon},\quad 0<\tilde\mu<2 .
\end{equation}
The reference $d[n]$ is the known training sequence for the first \prm{n_train} symbols and the
nearest constellation point afterwards (decision-directed mode). The residual MSE measured after
training is written to \meta{noise_var}.
\codeexcerpt{lms_loop}{The (N)LMS loop runs over time but processes all frames of the batch at once.}

\begin{keypoint}[ZF versus MMSE]
Zero forcing inverts spectral nulls and amplifies the noise there. MMSE balances ISI against
noise and tends to ZF at high SNR. The decision-feedback equalizer of \cref{sec:dfe} removes
post-cursor ISI without noise enhancement, at the cost of error propagation.
\end{keypoint}
